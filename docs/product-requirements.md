# VibeBuild Kenya — Product Requirements

## Product direction

VibeBuild Kenya is evolving from an aesthetic inspiration tool into a **local build-planning platform**. It helps a visitor convert an intended spend into a clear, creative plan: a chosen aesthetic, the right local vendor types, an indicative item or service mix, and a direct path to ask for availability. The product treats budget as a design constraint rather than a social signal. Every recommendation should feel intentional, specific, and attainable.

The MVP focuses on **fashion, personal styling, and small-space home refreshes** in Kenyan cities. It is not a marketplace or checkout product. It makes the decision process easier, then creates a structured introduction between a customer and a local creative or vendor.

| Product decision | VibeBuild approach |
| --- | --- |
| Core promise | Turn a real budget into a considered local plan, not a generic shopping list. |
| Primary audience | Style-aware people who want a clearer way to source outfits, tailoring, thrift finds, décor, and creative services locally. |
| Initial geography | Nairobi-first, with visible support for Mombasa, Kisumu, Nakuru, and online/delivery-capable vendors. |
| Trust posture | All preview inventory is explicitly marked as **demonstration data** until a vendor is verified and onboarded. |
| Commercial boundary | No checkout, payment, real-time inventory promise, or automatic vendor messaging in this MVP. |

## Core journeys

The public landing page explains the budget-to-build concept and opens a short brief. The brief asks for a budget, city, lifestyle category, aesthetic direction, and priorities. It returns an indicative build with a transparent cost breakdown and recommended vendor shortlist, including what has been assumed. Visitors can refine inputs without starting again.

Vendor discovery supports deliberate browsing when a visitor does not want a full recommendation. Search and filters surface vendors by service category, city, budget band, and compatible aesthetic. Each vendor page explains its point of view, sample services, indicative spend, social channels, and inquiry route without pretending stock is live.

Signed-in users can save build components and vendors to a personal build board. The board is a planning space rather than a cart. A user can create a public link for a build plan when they want to share it with friends, a stylist, or a vendor.

## Experience principles

The platform should feel like a confident editorial guide rather than a discount directory. Copy should be warm and exacting, with language such as **build**, **shape**, **source**, **consider**, and **make it yours**. It must never shame a budget or describe lower spend as a compromise.

The visual system is an elevated Nairobi editorial: warm paper ground, ink typography, amber and terracotta accents, strong photography crops, generous whitespace, fine rules, and a restrained motion system. The matching flow uses a clean progress narrative; recommendations use a moodboard-like composition, not checkout UI.

## MVP acceptance criteria

The MVP is successful when a visitor can submit a short brief, receive an understandable recommendation, filter to relevant vendors, inspect a vendor profile, save a plan after authentication, and send an inquiry that is stored with the selected vendor or build context. Every major asynchronous screen must have a clear loading, empty, and error treatment.
